Minister of Forestry, Fisheries and the Environment David Maynier has officially handed over a culvert infrastructure project at the Kruger National Park in Mpumalanga to SANParks. The culvert, located along the busy route between the Skukuza and Lower Sabie rest camps, was damaged during floods in January this year. Several roads, bridges and rest camps were also damaged by the floods. The floods in January this year caused extensive damage in the Kruger National Park, including to this culvert and other road infrastructure. However, following extensive consultation, repairs and collaboration, the work has now been completed and the road has officially been handed over to SANParks. The total extent of the damage was estimated at R950m. For the first time in seven months, tourists can once again use this popular stretch of road. The Kruger National Park was among the areas hardest hit by the devastating floods in January. The damage included roads, bridges and tourist camps, including Shimuwini Bushveld Camp, which was submerged. The total damage is estimated at close to R1 billion. Of this, R113 million has been allocated for structural damage, R196 million for bridges and culverts, and R640 million for tourist and park management roads. Authorities say some of the affected roads are critical for anti-poaching operations. SANParks CEO, Hapiloe Sello, says it will take several years to fully repair the damaged infrastructure. “We’re approaching the work in phases. Firstly, I think it’s about commending our colleagues to have come this far in a very short period of time. But if I recall from meetings that I have attended, it’ll take us perhaps five years, working at the same speed that we have to get our infrastructure back into working condition. And that will be in phases,” says Sello. R36 million was allocated by the national government to SANParks to support post-floods recovery. Minister of Forestry, Fisheries and Environment welcomed the progress made so far to repair the damage. “So recovery cannot simply mean replacing what was lost recovery requires better planning more resilient design and strategic investment into infrastructure that will last for years to come and withstand a future extreme weather events,” says Maynier. SANParks Week Maynier also officially launched SANParks Week , which starts on Monday. During the week, South African citizens are granted free access to most national parks for day visits. “South African National Parks Week provides an opportunity to families, to young people, to communities and to first time visitors to experience the beauty and the diversity and importance of our protected areas.
