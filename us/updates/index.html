What's new in Lootura

New features, improvements and changes to Lootura, version by version.

  1. 2.20.0-Ilargi14 nov 2026

    Catch up in your local time

    Catch up dates use your device’s time zone in both editions. The divider identifies the front page you had seen. Changes are marked as seen when you open this section and load the published version; leaving the site keeps pending changes. The bell has no dot when nothing is pending.

  2. 2.19.0-Elur14 nov 2026

    Changes beside the bell

    Unread changes appear beside the bell without opening a popup: E counts entities and N counts news articles. Yellow counters sit before the icon, whose dot gently pulses while changes are pending. Opening Catch up marks them as reviewed and clears the indicator. The animation respects reduced-motion preferences.

  3. 2.18.0-Eki14 nov 2026

    AI helps connect the news

    Cloudflare AI helps discover new entities and bring together headlines about the same event. Its candidates are checked against original source text, multiple publications, and identity and context rules before use. Publisher headlines and the existing Loot calculation are preserved. We have also reduced internal copies, compacted data and automatically clean unused caches to keep updates running.

  4. 2.17.0-Lur14 nov 2026

    Catch up at a glance

    Catch up groups headlines about the same story, just like the front page. Related links stay collapsed until you open their node. The more compact view keeps new changes separate from earlier entries and connects sources with lines and dots. The timeline ends with a dashed segment to show that it continues.

  5. 2.16.0-Naroa13 nov 2026

    Come back and catch up

    The bell opens Catch up: a timeline of related front-page links and entities with news from the past 48 hours. Changes since your last visit are separated from earlier entries, with amber dots for unread changes and empty circles for previous entries. When you return after a while and there are changes, a notice lets you view them, save them for later or turn off future notices. Your choice stays in this browser. The 90-day archive is still available from this section. We have also simplified the front page and menu by removing the scan countdown and Activity section.

  6. 2.15.0-Amaiur12 nov 2026

    Your first look around Lootura

    Take a guided look around the actual front page: discover entities, why a headline appears and how to follow updates. Then explore the main article, related sources, Archive and the menu. Skip it, go back or replay it from “How it works”. The guide adapts to mobile and remembers when you finish. We have also expanded our sources in Spain and the United States to follow more stories.

  7. 2.14.0-Mikel8 nov 2026

    The radar comes alive

    Open Activity and follow each scan step by step. The radar connects the stages: the active one pulses and completed ones turn green. Below, watch the counts of checked sources, processed links and newly discovered entities grow. A progress bar tracks completed stages, with the next scan on the right and start and finish times as they become available. Works on mobile and in dark mode too. And in What’s new, every character now has a Basque name.

  8. 2.13.0-Ane8 nov 2026

    Lootura, light or dark

    Choose how you read Lootura. In the menu, below Edition, you will find Theme: System, Light mode or Dark mode. System follows your device’s appearance; Light mode and Dark mode keep your choice. The website remembers it when you return or switch sections or editions. Dark mode keeps the photos, story timelines and yellow accents.

  9. 2.12.0-Maialen8 nov 2026

    See Lootura at work

    The front-page countdown now opens Activity: follow the scan stages and browse recent runs. The radar pulses yellow while working and turns white when finished. We are also starting a one-week AI trial to check whether articles cover the same event and their entities belong. We will compare the results with human review before drawing conclusions. During the trial, AI does not change the front page, Loots or entities.

  10. 2.11.0-Nerea8 nov 2026

    Your archive, your way

    Find a publication’s stories with the new Archive filter. The latest arrivals still come first, or you can group headlines by publication and reveal more as you read. Cover statistics now pair icons with numbers, including in the installed app. The countdown says “Next scan”: checking publications does not always bring new stories.

  11. 2.10.0-Iker7 nov 2026

    Suggestions from the Meneame community

    Your ideas shape the front page: people, places and organisations stand out through recent media coverage. Loots measure that attention and fade without fresh stories; repeated headlines contribute less. Below, stories and sources connect through lines and dots. Front page requires three distinct publications; Headlines requires two. Arrows show real changes when comparison data exists, and the Loots page explains the score.

  12. 2.9.0-Unai4 nov 2026

    Every headline, with its source

    Switching headlines now shows the matching publication, byline, date and link. Entities display their score, and arrows appear only when relevance changes. More sections keep the header in view as you explore.

  13. 2.8.0-Irati4 nov 2026

    A character for every release

    What’s new gets illustrated characters, one for each release. They stay the same between visits so you can recognise them.

  14. 2.7.0-Aitor4 nov 2026

    News worth finding again

    Meet the Archive: 90 days of articles you can search by headline, publication, author or topic. Each result shows when it arrived in Lootura and links to the original source. Find it below Directory in the menu.

  15. 2.6.0-Ander4 nov 2026

    Checking the news every two hours

    Lootura checks news from Spain and the United States every two hours. The front page changes when a new revision is available.

  16. 2.5.0-Jon2 nov 2026

    More room to read

    Front page and Headlines use the full mobile width. Arrows show how coverage changes, and the unread notice shows the age of the latest headline. The extension adds a recent-headlines tab ordered by date.

  17. 2.4.0-June1 nov 2026

    Switch lists without losing your place

    Front page and Headlines tabs stay visible when you switch lists. The header and notices leave room for the content you’re reading.

  18. 2.3.0-Iñaki1 nov 2026

    A lighter mobile cover

    Entities and publications share a line, with the countdown underneath. Statistics take up less room, while the total links analysed remains visible on desktop.

  19. 2.2.0-Leire1 nov 2026

    Entity pages that fit your phone

    Entity type, publications and articles share a compact row. A red icon marks special coverage. Extra navigation stays on desktop, leaving more room to read on mobile.

  20. 2.1.0-Xabier1 nov 2026

    Front page and Headlines stay close

    Tabs stay with you as you read, including on mobile. Coverage articles run from newest to oldest, and a countdown shows the next expected review. We also recover larger photos and preserve original descriptions for Press.

  21. 2.0.0-Gorka30 oct 2026

    Lootura 2.0: news with context

    The people, places and organisations in the news get their own space, with photos and headlines explaining why they matter now. Their pages bring links and timelines into Lootura. Spain and the United States share this experience on the web and in the installed app. Stories covered by eleven or more publications can trigger special coverage when they meet the selection criteria.

  22. 1.14.1-Amaia10 jul 2026

    Signal finds a home in Atlas

    Atlas adds an internal Signal view showing moving stories, supporting coverage and related entities. This view stays out of search engines.

  23. 1.14.0-Eneko10 jul 2026

    Atlas connects more of the story

    Atlas uses stories grouped across publications by Signal to discover entities and connect coverage. Quality checks still decide what gets published.

  24. 1.13.2-Ainhoa8 jul 2026

    The whole website, published together

    Every release rebuilds the whole website, even when only Signal or Atlas changes. It takes a little longer but avoids leaving parts of the site behind.

  25. 1.13.1-Markel8 jul 2026

    Entities keep their history

    An entity no longer disappears when its headlines change. Atlas keeps its identity and a short history so you can follow its progress.

  26. 1.13.0-Julen4 jul 2026

    Fewer broken photos

    We improved how article images are found. If a photo fails, Lootura uses a fallback background so a broken image doesn’t interrupt your reading.

  27. 1.12.0-Oier4 jul 2026

    Brief: a quick look at the news

    Meet Brief, a compact view of each edition’s main stories, available from the menu. The app and Chrome extension share the selection. Photos and long headlines also get a better fit.

  28. 1.11.0-Naia23 may 2026

    The same story, seen from abroad

    A new experiment shows how other countries cover stories from your edition. Results stay separated by edition and show the publication and byline when available.

  29. 1.10.0-Izaro20 may 2026

    Signals that tell the same story

    Headlines about the same event are grouped more accurately, even when publications describe it differently. Sharing a word is no longer enough to mix unrelated stories.

  30. 1.9.0-Garazi18 may 2026

    Japan joins Lootura

    A Japanese edition arrives alongside a simpler country selector. Labs focuses on four experiments, news languages stay better separated, and the desktop view gets lighter.

  31. 1.8.0-Oihane18 may 2026

    Three countries, more news

    South Africa, Mexico and Argentina join the edition selector. Each brings its own publications and stories, along with Signal and Labs.

  32. 1.7.0-Beñat17 may 2026

    Straight to the original story

    Tap a headline to open the original article. Save and share stay close, while related sources live in Signal and Labs. Each card has fewer labels.

  33. 1.6.0-Asier16 may 2026

    A closer look at bylines

    A new experiment shows which authors have been publishing more recently and what they cover. The extension can highlight active bylines. It measures activity, not the quality of their work.

  34. 1.5.0-Iñigo16 may 2026

    A place to try new ideas

    Meet Labs, a space to explore new ways of reading the news. Some hints appear beside headlines when supporting data exists. The installed app also gets a smoother loading screen.

  35. 1.4.0-Maddi14 may 2026

    Headlines return to the front page

    The front page returns to stories you can browse, save and share. Signal gets its own section, with a separate page for the words you follow.

  36. 1.3.0-Miren10 may 2026

    One story, several headlines

    Browse the main story’s related headlines, each with its own photo when available. Dots show where you are.

  37. 1.2.0-Uxue10 may 2026

    Stories that stay in the news

    The new 24H section brings together stories that remain active throughout the day. Open their sources and details without repeating what’s already on the front page.

  38. 1.1.0-Koldo2 may 2026

    Optional help for Signal

    An optional AI feature can improve Signal topic names and text using data already collected. It starts disabled, with a test mode and spending controls. If it fails, Signal continues as before.

  39. 1.0.0-Nahia1 may 2026

    Now, Rising and Falling

    Lootura moves to three views showing what’s happening and how media attention changes. Each topic opens its details and sources. These signals replace the classic news list.

  40. 0.23.0-Ibai30 apr 2026

    Radar tries new help, within limits

    An optional feature can improve Radar’s topic names. It can be tested without spending and given a budget. If it reaches the limit or fails, regular Radar keeps working.

  41. 0.22.0-Imanol28 apr 2026

    Radar follows your words

    The installed app adds Following: recent stories matching your chosen words, newest first. Matches disappear after no more than seven days.

  42. 0.21.0-Ekain28 apr 2026

    Choose what to follow

    In the installed app, Editions lets you choose a country, filter categories and follow words. In the browser, it remains a simple edition selector.

  43. 0.20.1-Aritz28 apr 2026

    Share from the story

    Each card adds a Share option. The link keeps a version of the story that can open even after the live list changes.

  44. 0.20.0-Urko27 apr 2026

    Links that keep the story

    Shared links preserve the basic details of a story or Radar topic. Recipients can open them even after they leave the live feed.

  45. 0.19.0-Peru27 apr 2026

    A tidier My Lootura

    Saved stories, muted sources and interests each get a tab. Clearing saved stories now asks for confirmation and keeps your other preferences.

  46. 0.18.0-Hodei27 apr 2026

    When a source fails, we wait

    Lootura keeps useful recent data and spaces out retries when a source fails. The internal quality screen explains whether a publication is quiet, returns no stories or reports an error.

  47. 0.17.0-Edurne27 apr 2026

    Reddit conversations come to Radar

    US Radar topics can include related Reddit threads. They help you explore the conversation but don’t count as publications or trigger topics on their own.

  48. 0.16.0-Aimar27 apr 2026

    Inside Lootura

    Meet Stackroom, an internal section explaining how news arrives and how the app works. Open it from What’s new.

  49. 0.15.0-Lorea27 apr 2026

    A little calm, from the menu

    Turn on Calm mode beside the dark-mode setting. It reduces breaking news, live coverage and repetitive politics without switching editions.

  50. 0.14.0-Itsaso27 apr 2026

    Your space in Lootura

    Saved becomes My Lootura. Review stories, unmute publications and remove interests you no longer want to follow. Everything stays on your device.

  51. 0.13.0-Lander27 apr 2026

    Shape your news with a swipe

    In the installed app, swipe left for more stories like this. Swipe right to mute the publication for 24 hours. Your preferences stay on your device.

  52. 0.12.0-Eñaut27 apr 2026

    A quick guide to swiping

    The installed app shows a short introduction to sideways swipes. It appears only once and leaves room to keep reading.

  53. 0.11.0-Itziar27 apr 2026

    Catch up when you return

    After time away, the installed app summarises new stories and Radar changes. Keep reading, save the summary for later or turn it off.

  54. 0.10.0-Oihan27 apr 2026

    See how reliably a source arrives

    A label near the byline shows whether Lootura receives that publication’s news regularly or intermittently. It describes the source connection, not the article’s truthfulness.

  55. 0.9.0-Alaia26 apr 2026

    Saved stories last 15 days before being removed automatically. The refreshed screen makes that limit and its controls clearer.

  56. 0.8.0-Haizea26 apr 2026

    A closer look at quality

    The internal Status screen adds a tab for checking troubled sources and poorly grouped topics. Data checks before publishing are also stronger.

  57. 0.7.0-Danel26 apr 2026

    Lootura can highlight one world anniversary each day. If no date is relevant enough, none appears.

  58. 0.6.0-Aner26 apr 2026

  59. 0.5.0-Unax25 apr 2026

    Dark mode arrives across the app. Lootura follows your device setting, and you can also change it from the menu. Your choice stays saved for future visits.

  60. 0.4.0-Luken25 apr 2026

    Radar sharpens its hearing

    Editions stay better separated, and headlines need clearer matches before being grouped. If there’s no solid topic, Radar leaves the space empty.

  61. 0.3.0-Saioa23 apr 2026

    Switching on Radar

    Radar groups headlines about the same story to help you spot the day’s topics. It updates without reloading the whole app and flags new activity in the installed version.

  62. 0.2.0-Olatz23 apr 2026

    More of the world to discover

    More news and culture publications join the feed, with a better article mix and short editorial features. Stories also stay available longer.

  63. 0.1.0-Eider22 apr 2026

    We start with a simple prototype: full-screen stories you browse by swiping. At the end, you can return to the beginning.
