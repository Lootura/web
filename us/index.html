    Republicans warily eye Trump’s meeting with Putin, Xi Semafor · 4.7 LOOTS contributed
    The News Republican senators are casting a wary eye on President Donald Trump’s high-stakes Thursday summit with Chinese leader Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin. Trump arrived in Shenzhen on Wednesday, where Xi greeted him warmly on the tarmac. The three leaders are expected to hold a trilateral summit on Thursday. “I’d rather see a summit with our NATO members talking about how we’re going to beat Russia,” Sen. Thom Tillis, R-N.C., told Semafor when asked about the meeting. Sen. John Cornyn, R-Texas, who lost his primary to Trump-backed challenger Ken Paxton, struck a more measured tone — but added a warning. “It’s important that the president engage with our principal adversaries around the world. I just hope he doesn’t give away too much,” the Texas Republican said. Sen. Rick Scott, R-Fla., similarly expressed support for the diplomatic effort, though he acknowledged it is unlikely to produce results. “I’m glad he’s meeting with them. I know that’s what he does,” Scott said. “But, I don’t expect anything to happen, because these are two thugs that hate our way of life.” Nvidia scandal clouds meeting Others implored Trump to confront Xi over Chinese espionage. Sen. Jim Banks, R-Ind., praised the president for being “the toughest negotiator we’ve ever had,” but said he hoped Trump would raise concerns about Chinese intelligence operations. He pointed to the recent controversy swirling around Nvidia, the chipmaker that saw its CEO Jensen Huang step down last week amid revelations of his affair with a suspected Chinese spy. “I hope he pushes President Xi on these Chinese espionage issues. They’re obviously targeted at America,” Banks said. “This is a very serious issue. Why is President Xi allowing for Chinese spies to perform operations against our top American CEOs? Those are big questions.” Tillis also questioned whether the circumstances surrounding the Nvidia scandal made for a productive diplomatic atmosphere. “We’ve sent people on Air Force One to Beijing, only to find out that one of them may have been a Chinese asset spying against us,” Tillis said. “How does that serve as a basis for a good, you know, happy meeting? Let’s be realistic.” Room for Disagreement Still, not all Republicans were skeptical of the summit: Sen. Steve Daines, R-Mont., praised the “historic meeting,” describing the relationship between the US and China as “too big to fail.” The two countries represent “42% of the world economy” and are “both now nuclear superpowers,” Daines said. “I’m glad he’s doing it.”
